# Gap register

Status: current as of 2026-08-30. Every entry cites the note it comes from, and nothing here is a gap someone thought of in the abstract. There are three classes, because they call for three different responses.

- *L — gaps in the published literature.* Not ours to close. These are the openings the prototype occupies, and the thing to watch is whether someone else fills them first.
- *E — evidence we could retrieve and have not.* Ours to close, cheaply, when a decision depends on it.
- *D — gaps no amount of reading closes.* Settled by a decision, or blocked by a project constraint. They are listed so they aren't mistaken for reading debt.

*If you act on one thing, make it [the assumed material factors](#assumed-material-factors).* The finding it underwrites — that stainless costs more carbon than glass — is the one the footprint interaction design is built around, and it could invert. [The unread grid factor](#unread-grid-factor) is one download. One other entry bears on a decision in flight: [unobserved configurator behaviour](#unobserved-configurator-behaviour) is one browser session, and it is the only evidence anyone could bring to the question of whether a solver-refused option should stay disabled. Nothing else in this register changes a build decision today.

| <a id="assumed-material-factors"></a>*The assumed material factors* | Stainless steel at about 5.5, rubber at about 3.2 and PVC at about 3.1 kg CO₂e/kg are *assumed* rather than retrieved.
