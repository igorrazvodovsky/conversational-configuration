# What a suggestion chip should do when it is clicked

Status: retrieved 2026-08-30; its recommendation was taken the same day, and the entry prompts were reworded ([suggested moves](../specs/suggested-moves/design.md), decision 2). Answers one question — whether clicking a suggested prompt should send it or place it in the composer for the customer to edit — and reads the answer against the [suggested moves](../specs/suggested-moves/requirements.md) strip as built.

*The evidence is transfer rather than direct measurement, and the note is written on that basis.* No study was found that compares click-to-send against click-to-insert for chat suggestion chips, and the two chat-side pattern libraries that name the pattern do not rule on it. What is solid is adjacent and peer-reviewed: what accepting model-authored text does to what a person writes and believes, and the expected-utility account of when a system should act rather than ask. The shipped behaviour below is *retrieved* from vendor documentation and trade coverage, not observed hands-on, which is registered as a gap.

## Verdict

The literature supports neither a blanket switch to prefill nor the strip's current send-on-click. Both bodies of evidence separate on the same line, and it is not the line the question is usually asked along: what matters is whether the chip *asserts something about the customer's own situation that they have not said*, not whether the chip is long, complex or consequential.

- A chip that names a move the customer is making — *Make it cheaper*, *Why this service level?* — asserts nothing. Sending it on click is right, and the evidence for asking first is absent.
- A chip that authors facts — the entry prompt that sends "a new 6-storey hotel in Munich, about 20 m of travel, normal guest traffic" over a label reading *Hotel new build* — puts a project description in the customer's mouth that they never read.

That line falls exactly where [suggested moves](../specs/suggested-moves/design.md) already draws one: a move pill's title *is* its message, and the five entry prompts are the standing exception where it is not. So the question is narrower than it looks. It is a question about five chips.

*Prefill is not the strongest answer to it, and Jakesch et al. is why.* Editability does not neutralize the influence of model-authored text — participants who edited suggestions were shifted by them anyway (*What accepting model-authored text does*). What prefill buys is that the assertion becomes visible before it lands, which is worth something and is not the same thing as making the words the customer's own. The option that removes the fabricated facts rather than displaying them first is to reword the entry chips so they open the subject and let the agent ask, which is what this repo already does twice — in *Start from our RFQ* and in the *answer the document* family — and what costs nothing to extend.

That matters here more than it would elsewhere, because prefill is not reachable through the installed CopilotKit at all without replacing the connected chat component (*What this prototype can actually do*).

## 1. What accepting model-authored text does

Three peer-reviewed results, all about text a person accepts from a model and may then edit. None is about a chat suggestion chip; the transfer is stated where it is weakest.

*Editing does not neutralize the influence.* Jakesch et al., [CHI 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3581196), had 1,506 participants write a post about whether social media is good for society, with a GPT-3 assistant steered to argue one side. Suggestions appeared inline in grey after a pause and were accepted word by word with tab or an accept button, so a participant could take part of a sentence and write the rest. Most did: 63% of sentences were written without accepting anything, 25% were co-written, and only 11.5% were fully accepted (*retrieved*, §4.2). The influence survived that editing. Participants with the pro-social-media model were 2.04 times more likely than the control group to argue social media is good (p<0.0001, 95% CI [1.83, 2.30]), and those with the critical model 2.0 times more likely to argue the opposite (95% CI [1.79, 2.24]); the shift then showed up in a post-task attitude survey, where 45% of the pro group answered that social media is good against 35% of the control group, and 41% of the critical group answered that it is not against 33% of the control (*retrieved*, §§4.1–4.3). The authors' own description of the mechanism is the one that transfers: the assistant "augmented rather than replaced their narrative". A prefilled sentence the customer trims is still the model's sentence.

*Suggestions narrow what gets said.* Arnold, Chauncey and Gajos, [IUI 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3377325.3377523), compared image captions written with and without predictive text and found captions written with suggestions were shorter and contained fewer words the system had not predicted (*retrieved* from [the authors' summary](https://medium.com/harvard-hci/predictive-text-systems-change-what-we-write-5eb75dc9fb0d); the paper's figures were not read). Their conclusion is that a text-entry system should be evaluated by its effect on what gets written, not only on speed. For this prototype that is a claim about elicitation: an entry chip that supplies a building is supplying the description instead of hearing one.

*A step that forces engagement works, and is disliked.* Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos, [CSCW 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692), found cognitive forcing functions — interventions requiring the person to engage before the AI's answer is available — reduced overreliance on incorrect AI suggestions, and reported a trade-off: participants preferred and trusted the systems they found less mentally demanding, and the interventions helped participants high in need for cognition more than others. Cox et al.'s [microboundaries](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/uclic/sites/uclic/files/design-frictions_chi2016lbw_v14.pdf) name the general form: a small deliberate boundary that moves a person from automatic to reflective action. Prefill is a microboundary. So the case for it is not that it is free, and the customer in the [delivery lead's](../discovery/jtbd/persona-delivery-lead.md) position is exactly the one who will find the extra keystroke tedious on the chips that assert nothing.

## 2. When a system should act rather than ask

Horvitz, [CHI 1999](https://erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf), gives the framing the question actually needs. Comparing the expected utility of acting with that of not acting yields a threshold probability p\* on the system's belief that the user holds the goal: act above it, refrain below. Adding a third option — *dialog*, asking the user about the goal — yields two thresholds instead, one between inaction and dialog and one between dialog and action, because asking is better than acting when the user does not hold the goal, and worse than acting when they do (*retrieved*, *Expected Utility and Thresholds for Agent Action*, *Dialog as an Option for Action*).

Read onto the strip, the pill *is* the dialog rung: the customer has already been asked, and clicking is the answer. What the entry prompts do is different — they answer for the customer, on a question about their own building, and the answer is not shown on the chip. That is acting above a threshold the system has no evidence for, and Horvitz's principle about the cost of poor guesses is the one it violates. Prefill moves those five chips from action back to dialog. Rewording them does the same thing more cheaply, by making the chip's sentence a question the agent then asks.

## 3. What ships

Read from vendor documentation and trade coverage, not exercised.

| Product | On click | What that says |
|---|---|---|
| Gmail Smart Reply ([Gmail Help](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/17493601)) | Inserts into the reply draft: "select a suggestion below the message. You can edit the suggestion before you reply. Then click Send" | Two gestures, and the editing step is documented as part of the flow rather than as a recovery from it |
| Google Messages Smart Reply ([9to5Google, 2026-01-16](https://9to5google.com/2026/01/16/google-messages-smart-reply-edit/)) | Sends. A *Tap to Edit* setting in beta changes it to update the draft instead | The same vendor ships both behaviours for the same pattern and is making it a user setting rather than a ruling. The reasons the coverage reports are personalizing before sending and preventing accidental sends |
| Microsoft 365 Copilot Prompt Gallery ([Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/copilot-prompt-gallery), [Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/topic/edit-a-copilot-prompt-to-make-it-your-own-4f766981-dd4c-4038-a025-0f88c67fd9db)) | The prompt is a template carrying bracketed slots — `[topic]`, `[file]`, `[your title]` — the user fills before submitting, and any other part is editable too | Where a suggested prompt must carry specifics only the user knows, the shipped answer is a visible slot rather than an invented value |
| Search autocomplete ([Smart Interface Design Patterns](https://smart-interface-design-patterns.com/articles/autocomplete-ux/)) | Two affordances on one row: clicking the suggestion runs the search, a secondary control appends it to the box so the query can be built on | A decades-old resolution of exactly this question, and it resolves it by shipping both rather than by choosing |
| Suggested prompts in chat, per [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-chatbots-design-guidelines/) | Send. The guidance is to present them as buttons so the user can "start the conversation by simply clicking" | The advice is about removing the burden of formulating a question, and is silent on editing after selection |
| [Shape of AI](https://www.shapeof.ai/patterns/nudges) *Nudges* | Unruled | The pattern library names when and where to show a nudge and does not address what a click does |
| Nielsen, [prompt augmentation](https://www.uxtigers.com/post/prompt-augmentation) | Both, as separate patterns | Related prompts stand apart from the input; prompt rewrite puts text *in* the input on the argument that editing existing text is easier than authoring it |

*What the table does not separate on is how costly the send is.* Gmail and Google Messages are the same vendor and the same pattern, and a text message is as irrevocable as an email, yet one inserts and the other sends — and rather than rule, Google is making it a setting. Length does not separate them either; both suggest a short canned phrase. Where the products do agree is on the case this prototype has: wherever a suggestion carries specifics only the user knows, the answer is insertion or a visible slot, never an invented value. That is the prompt gallery's brackets and autocomplete's append control, and it is exactly what the five entry prompts do not do.

## 4. What this prototype can actually do

*Prefill is not reachable through `CopilotChat`.* Measured against the installed `@copilotkit/react-core` 1.65.0 by reading the bundle, not the docs, on the same basis as [the CopilotKit surface note](copilotkit-surface.md):

- `handleSelectSuggestion` adds the suggestion's message to the agent and starts a run. Send-on-click is the component's behaviour, not a default the strip chose.
- `onSelectSuggestion` is typed *out* of `CopilotChatProps` and is nonetheless reachable at runtime, because the component builds its props with the handler first and spreads the caller's props after it. Overriding it means depending on a spread order the types disclaim.
- `inputValue` and `onInputChange` are typed *into* `CopilotChatProps` and are then overridden unconditionally at render, after the caller's props are spread. Passing them type-checks and silently does nothing. This is the same class of failure as the three silent ones [the stylesheet](../specs/ui-component-library/design.md) warns about, and it is worth recording whatever happens to the strip.

So writing to the composer means giving up `CopilotChat` for `CopilotChatView`, which the library supports — the view takes `suggestions`, `onSelectSuggestion`, `inputValue` and `onInputChange`, and `useSuggestions` is exported — at the price of re-implementing what the connected component owns: the run and stop handling, attachments, transcription, the suggestion lifecycle and the welcome-screen gating. That is a large change to buy a smaller one, and it is the strongest practical argument for rewording the chips instead.

*Prefill does not collide with one gesture, one action.* That feature rules what a *dispatched* change reaches — every `Set …` sentence reaching `revise_choices`, so a picked change applies whole or comes back with repair paths ([one gesture, one action](../specs/one-gesture-one-action/design.md)). A prefilled chip dispatches nothing; it is a gesture with no action until the customer sends. The pills already stay outside that grammar by [suggested moves](../specs/suggested-moves/design.md)'s fourth decision, and prefill would not change which tool anything reaches.

*Send-then-edit is not an alternative here.* CopilotKit renders an edit control on user messages, but its behaviour is the application's to supply, and editing a sent message means re-running from that point. Tools write through to the workspace store as they run, so a re-run re-applies actions against a store that already recorded them. The [agreement workspace](../specs/agreement-workspace/design.md) is what rules that out.

*Prefill would invalidate the catalogue's repeat suppression.* `suggestedMoves` is a pure function of the configuration, whether anything has been said, and the set of sentences the customer has already *sent* verbatim. Two families — *ask why* and *revise by intent* — use that set as the only thing that stops them re-offering a question forever. A prefilled sentence the customer edits before sending never matches, so those two families would re-offer questions already asked in altered words. The entry prompts read none of that, which is a further reason any change should stop at them.

## 5. What this note does not establish

- *Whether prefill would change what customers say here.* Nothing in the evidence measures the chat-chip case, and the transfer from writing assistants is an assumption of the kind [carbon labelling is all food](gaps.md#carbon-labelling-is-all-food) names for the footprint work. It is registered as [no comparison of chip dispatch](gaps.md#no-chip-dispatch-comparison).
- *What the major assistants actually do on click.* Documentation and trade coverage do not say, and no product was exercised. One browser session closes it, and it is registered as [unobserved chip behaviour](gaps.md#unobserved-chip-behaviour).
- *Whether a two-affordance chip works in chat.* Search shipped it, and no chat product surveyed has. Recommending it here would be extrapolating from one genre to another with nothing in between.
- *Whether the entry prompts are read at all before they are clicked.* The complaint that opened the [suggested moves](../specs/suggested-moves/requirements.md) spec was that they persisted, not that they misrepresented anyone. Nobody has been observed clicking one and being surprised by what it sent.
