# Consumption journey — buying, using, adjusting, renewing

Status: desk-research hypothesis. Play: *Map the Consumption Journey*, covering how the offering is discovered, adopted and kept. It is distinct from the [job map](job-map.md), which models the job itself.

realize need → explore options → configure and negotiate → commit → install and onboard → operate → adjust → renew or exit

## The product frame against the service frame

Under the product frame the supplier's journey effectively ends at *commit*, and everything after is a different department and a different contract. Under the service frame the configuration becomes the living core of the agreement: the configured outcome terms — handling capacity, uptime level, response time, term, monthly price — persist through operate, adjust and renew, and mid-contract reconfiguration is a normal event rather than a sales exception.

Two journey stages become configurable under the service frame. At *adjust*, you change the outcome terms and see the ripple. At *renew*, you resume the live configuration and revise it against actual usage. This is why the living-document concept has no start and no finish ([direction](../direction.md), *Concept*): joining a document already in some state is what mid-contract revision and renewal already are.

## Hypothesized friction worth designing for

- At *explore*, comparing a capital purchase against a service subscription is genuinely hard.
- At *configure*, the articulation barrier bites ([design specifier](persona-design-specifier.md)).
- At *adjust*, fear of lock-in bites: opaque change costs are the service-model version of vendor lock.
