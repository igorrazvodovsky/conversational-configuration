# Job map — the main job as eight stages

Status: desk-research hypothesis. Play: *Map the Main Job*, using the universal stages as a prompt, from the performer's perspective and free of solution language.

| Stage | The performer's goal |
|---|---|
| Define | Establish what the building needs — traffic, accessibility, code regime — triggered by a new build, a conversion, or an aging installation |
| Locate | Gather building data, budget frame, candidate suppliers and delivery models |
| Prepare | Translate needs into something feasible, and align architect, contractor, tenants and board |
| Confirm | Validate feasibility, compliance and cost, and secure approval and financing |
| Execute | Contract, install, commission, hand over |
| Monitor | Track uptime, ride quality, complaints and cost of ownership |
| Modify | Adapt to changed use: traffic growth, repurposing, modernization, upgrades |
| Conclude | Renew, replace or exit at end of contract or equipment life |

## The observation that drives everything downstream

A *product* configurator serves Define through Confirm and then exits. Monitor, Modify and Conclude — where most of the job's lifetime cost and frustration sit — are left to a separate maintenance contract the performer negotiates blind. A *service* offering keeps the configuration alive through those stages, and the prototype's [nonlinear-revision machinery](../../specs/nonlinear-interaction/requirements.md) — revision with ripple, candidate frames, resumption — is a Modify and Conclude instrument that the product frame leaves unused.

This is a map of the job rather than of the buying experience, which is the [consumption journey](consumption-journey.md).
