Status: drafted 2026-08-20, for critique. It answers *how a two-objective trade-off between two drafts is read, and where the reading happens* — [phase-plan.md](../phase-plan.md) §3 task 3, the exploring gap §1 names. The shipped chat card is candidate one, drawn from observation rather than from its source, and two alternatives are drawn against it. Judged against [trade-offs are shown as a pair, not collapsed into a score](../principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md), whose own test is the criterion: after seeing the comparison, can the reader say which options differ and what each costs?

## 0. Scope, and what was in front of it

The mechanism exists and is not in question. `compare_drafts` returns only the differing variables, each side priced at its own contract term, with the monthly delta and the footprint delta beside each other ([parallel-drafts](../../specs/parallel-drafts/design.md)); the deltas are computed backend-side and are valid by construction. What has never been chosen is the *placement and layout* of the result, which arrived in chat because a tool result had somewhere to go there.

The drawings below were made against a real workspace rather than from imagination: *Munich office — 12 floors*, three drafts, comparing *Premium service* (€1,182/mo, 36.2 t CO₂e) against *Original* (€1,053/mo, 37.8 t CO₂e), fourteen differences. Sizes are from that comparison in the running app at a 1600 × 1000 viewport. Fourteen is not a stress case: it is what two drafts of one small agreement differ by when one of them changed the service level.

## 1. Candidate one — the card in the transcript, as it behaves

A table inside a chat message: a header row naming the two drafts and marking which is current, one row per differing variable with each side's value and its own monthly adder, then totals, modelled footprint, a one-line delta sentence, and a *Work on Premium service* button.

The content is close to right. Only differences are listed, both objectives are present and unresolved, and each side is priced at its own term. The failures are all consequences of *where it is*, and each was observed rather than predicted.

*It does not fit the surface it lives on.* The table is 620 px in a 659 px scroller. Scrolled so the totals and the switch button are visible, the header row naming which column is which sits 347 px above the viewport; scrolled so the header is visible, the totals and the button are below it. At fourteen differences the reader can see what the columns *are* or what they *come to*, not both. The principle's test asks for both at once.

*The geometry the user chose can make it much worse or remove it entirely.* [chat-surface](../../specs/chat-surface/design.md) ships four geometries. In floating mode the same table is 310 px wide and 972 px tall in a 442 px window — 2.2 screens of scrolling for one comparison, while 1100 px of canvas sits empty beside it. In hidden mode the table is still in the DOM, inside the chat's `inert` wrapper at zero opacity, and the canvas takes the whole surface showing one of the two drafts with no trace that a comparison was ever made and no way to bring it back. A comparison the user cannot reach is not a placement choice the user made; it is a placement choice the app made for them.

*It expires at the first question about it.* A card goes inert once any user message follows the message carrying its tool call ([card-dispatch.ts](../../../src/components/generative-ui/card-dispatch.ts)). Asking *why is the premium draft's footprint lower when it costs more?* — the most ordinary move there is after seeing a pair of figures that disagree — disabled *Work on Premium service* and dimmed the card to 0.6 opacity. The comparison is a thing you decide *with*, and interrogating it is part of deciding; the card treats interrogation as having moved on. Then the answer streamed in and pushed the card out of view, so the reader was left with prose about *this comparison* and no comparison on screen.

*It duplicates the canvas and is duplicated by the chat.* The left half of the screen was already rendering *Original* in the agreement genre — recitals, numbered terms, provenance, collapsed schedules — while the card rendered the same draft again as a flat row of catalogue nomenclature ("Car dimensions (width × depth)", "Shaft size"). One agreement, two genres, on one screen, and the genre that was chosen for the project ([Canvas anatomy](Canvas%20anatomy.md)) is not the one doing the comparing. Underneath, the agent then narrated the whole card in prose and offered to *show the two drafts side by side* — having just done so — which is the chat carrying state that the canvas is supposed to hold ([the canvas holds the state and the chat explains it](../principles/canvas-holds-state-chat-explains.md)).

The [comparing agreements](../scenarios/comparing-agreements.md) scenario already carries this as a working hypothesis: a pair of figures read in a scrolling transcript may not be a pair the reader can hold. It is no longer a hypothesis.

## 2. Candidate two — a comparison mode of the canvas

The canvas gains a third mode beside the document and the render ([visual-configuration](../../specs/visual-configuration/design.md) established that a mode is an available move here, and that a second genre of the same state costs nothing when it is a pure projection). Entering it splits the document into two columns of the same genre, both drafts named at the head of their column and pinned there, with the differing terms rendered in place and everything they agree on collapsed to a single line saying how much is identical. Totals and footprint sit at the foot of each column as the *Consideration* clause already does. Taking a side is the switch it already is.

The layering is the one the [Ripple storyboard](Ripple%20storyboard.md) §6 handed forward as a hypothesis: differences first, whole documents one move down. Here the second half is reachable rather than figurative, because both sides are full drafts — expanding a column gives the whole agreement in its own genre, which is the sheet the reader already knows how to read.

What this buys is exactly what candidate one loses. The comparison survives the chat being hidden, because it is not in the chat. It survives a question about itself, because nothing on the canvas expires when the transcript moves — the canvas is the durable locus of state ([the canvas is the durable locus of state](../assertions/canvas-is-the-durable-state.md)), and a comparison the user is deciding from belongs to the same class of object as the pending revision and repair set that §3 of the storyboard already moved document-side for the same reason. It has the width to put a header and a total on one screen. And it compares the agreement in the agreement's own words.

What it costs: two columns of prose recitals at a 1600 px viewport give each side roughly 700 px, which is narrow for prose, and below `lg` the columns have nowhere to go. The answer is not that recitals rarely differ — in the comparison drawn here three of the values the recitals prose speaks did differ. It is that they are not the recitals layer's own. The layer maps onto the model's `context` group and owns four variables: building type, region, installation type and accessibility package ([configurator.ts](../../../src/lib/configurator.ts), `LAYER_OF_GROUP`). Everything else the prose says — the service level, the term, the usage profile, the connectivity package — is restatement of `agreement`-group values that are also numbered operative terms, and those three of them that differed here appear as terms 1, 2 and 4. So the recitals are set once, and the pairs appear where the terms are numbered. Only a difference in one of the four context variables makes the recitals themselves differ — a fork that changes the installation type would — and that is a difference row like any other rather than a second column of prose.

## 3. Candidate three — the redline

One document, the current draft, in its own genre, with each differing term carrying the other draft's value inline against it, struck or annotated — the way a contract is actually compared in procurement, and a form the reader may already know from their own tooling.

It reads well and it needs no new mode. It is rejected for two reasons, and the second is the stronger.

The form is asymmetric: it frames one draft as the document and the other as a set of proposed changes to it, so the natural verb is *accept*. But two drafts are symmetric — each is a whole agreement with its own provenance and history, neither is a proposal against the other, and the move is *switch*, which loses nothing on either side ([parallel-drafts](../../specs/parallel-drafts/requirements.md)). A form that makes one side primary invites the reader to answer a question the state does not pose.

And the redline is already spoken for, by something that *is* asymmetric. The deviation register renders the customer's own tender against the live agreement, term by term, with the counterparty's clause and quote attached ([rfq-reconciliation](../../specs/rfq-reconciliation/design.md)). That relationship really does have a primary document and a set of departures from it, and answering one really is negotiation rather than bookkeeping — which is why reconciliation dispatches its own sentence and not an edit. Giving draft comparison the same visual form would make two different relationships look like one, on the same canvas, in the same session. The two forms should stay distinguishable precisely because the moves they license differ.

## 4. The decision

*Candidate two, with candidate one demoted to narration.* The comparison is a canvas mode; the chat says at most one sentence when it opens one, and the reader is not asked to hold a pair of figures in a scrolling transcript.

Why this and not the others: it is the only candidate that survives all four chat geometries and survives being asked about, it is the only one that compares the agreement in the genre the project chose for the agreement, and it keeps the surface division the direction already commits to, with the canvas holding what is being decided and the chat explaining why. Candidate one is not wrong about *what* to show — its content is retained wholesale — only about where. Candidate three is a good form attached to the wrong relationship, and it is needed intact for the relationship it does fit.

*What happens to `compare_drafts`' card.* The tool keeps its name, its arguments and its computed payload; what moves is the payload's *consumer*. `DraftComparison` is a card — it parses the payload for `kind: "draft_comparison"`, dims on staleness through `useCardDispatch`, and dispatches a switch sentence from a button. A canvas mode is none of those things: it is a projection of workspace state that the payload opens, switching through the canvas's own chrome, with no staleness of its own. So the card component retires rather than moves, and the agent's accompanying text shrinks to the delta sentence the card's footer already carries. This is the same treatment [agreement-document](../../specs/agreement-document/design.md) gives a clean canvas edit — the sheet is the record, the chat carries only consequences — with the difference that a comparison is worth one visible sentence, because the agent opening one unasked is a move the customer should see it make ([Conversation moves](Conversation%20moves.md) §1). Two callers depend on this and are settled by it: the [suggested-moves](../../specs/suggested-moves/design.md) *fork and compare* pill now has the surface it was waiting on and may be offered, and the tool's docstring, which currently promises the customer a side-by-side card, has to describe a canvas view instead. That docstring is read by the agent, so rewording it is a system-prompt change, and the only check that sees one is `compare_refs.py` ([conversation-checks](../../specs/conversation-checks/design.md)), which costs money — the verification the implementation will need, not something this note runs.

*What stays open.* Whether the first proposal should arrive as a pair rather than as a disclosed cheapest-completion default ([Conversation moves](Conversation%20moves.md) §6, and the same question recorded in [trade-offs are shown as a pair](../principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md)) was waiting on this model for a place to put the pair. It now has one, and the question becomes answerable rather than answered: a two-column canvas can hold a cost/carbon pair at first proposal, but whether opening on two drafts helps a customer who has stated nothing yet is a separate reading, and it belongs with the session map.

## 5. Failure signals for the walkthrough

For [phase-plan.md](../phase-plan.md) §3 task 6, this model predicts its own refutations:

- The reader expands both columns to full documents every time, and reads the difference list not at all → the differences-first layering is wrong for comparison even though it was right for ripple, and the whole-document view is the default.
- The reader switches drafts back and forth to compare rather than using the comparison → two columns do not in fact hold a pair, and the thing being tested is [two objectives held as a pair make the trade-off legible](../assertions/two-objectives-as-a-pair.md) rather than the layout.
- The reader asks in chat which one is better → the pair is legible and the refusal to score it is the complaint, which is the principle under attack rather than the model.
- Comfort with two columns at fourteen differences must not be recorded as evidence for the layout at platform scale; the same caveat the ripple storyboard attaches to full-set disclosure applies here, and hundreds of variables differ differently from fourteen.

## 6. Defects observed while drawing this

Not this model's subject. All three were fixed the same day, none needed a spec (constitution #13), and the two that changed the agreement's own behaviour are recorded in [parallel-drafts](../../specs/parallel-drafts/design.md).

- *The switcher offered a move that errors.* `compare_drafts` needs a priced candidate on both sides and the menu offered *Compare with X* unconditionally. The item is now disabled carrying *no price*, and collapses to a single disabled *Compare — no price* when the draft being worked on is the unpriced one. Both branches run in the app.
- *One figure, two roundings.* The card said 1.5 t CO₂e where the agent's sentence beside it said 1,529 kg CO2e, having read the raw delta out of the payload. The payload now carries `footprintDeltaText`, formatted by the `_format_co2` that exists for exactly this, and the card renders it. Unit-tested both ways, including the pre-footprint fallback.
- *The agent restated the card and offered a move it had just made.* The prompt already forbids repeating a table; it did not forbid offering to show what is already shown. The tool's docstring now carries the same instruction `undo_change`'s does — say what it means and stop — and the prompt gains one line: never offer a move you have just made.

The third fix, and the docstring half of the second, are prose behaviour, so neither is verified here: `compare_refs.py` is the only check that sees a docstring or prompt edit, it costs money, and running it was not part of this task ([conversation-checks](../../specs/conversation-checks/design.md)).
