Status: drafted 2026-08-13, for critique. It answers *how much of the consequence set to show, and in what form* — the ripple-disclosure question in [problem framing](../problem-framing.md), *Open questions the framing does not settle* — by drawing one revision frame by frame, with the moment of divergence rendered at each candidate disclosure level, the built level among them. This is the storyboard of the project's central claim, [showing the ripple at the moment of revision makes nonlinear change workable](../assertions/ripple-at-the-moment-of-revision.md): nonlinear revision becomes workable when the ripple is shown at the moment of revision, with repairs proposed.

## 0. Scenario and scope

One revision: the *mid-contract revision* [job story](../jtbd/job-stories.md), with the building-operator persona. The spine deliberately matches the [mid-contract revision](../scenarios/mid-contract-revision.md) scenario and the modernization example in [nonlinear interaction](../../specs/nonlinear-interaction/design.md), so the storyboard describes an interaction the code can almost already run. The outcome-terms layer is the [service agreement](../../specs/service-agreement/requirements.md)'s, live on the canvas. The footprint deltas use the data of the [footprint spec](../../specs/environmental-footprint/requirements.md), implemented 2026-08-13. The hardware ripple uses the live R-ids from `agent/src/product_model/elevator.json`, and the numbers are illustrative. The geometry follows [Surface architecture](Surface%20architecture.md): the chat-plus-document split, static cards, and repair sets as document-side artifacts. That last one is a pending amendment to the [canvas](../../specs/agreement-document/requirements.md) and [nonlinear-interaction](../../specs/nonlinear-interaction/requirements.md) specs, drawn here as the target.

Opening state: a live agreement, mid-term. Installation type *modernization*, rated speed *1.6 m/s*, outcome terms including a peak wait target, monthly price and footprint attached, provenance tags throughout.

## 1. The frames

*F1 — Re-entry.* The operator reopens the agreement. On the canvas, the document is exactly as left ([the canvas is the durable locus of state](../assertions/canvas-is-the-durable-state.md), with resumption per [nonlinear interaction](../../specs/nonlinear-interaction/requirements.md)), with no onboarding and no start ([configuration can start from any variable, in any order](../principles/start-from-any-variable.md)). The chat is idle, or answers "where were we?" from state. No move has been made.

*F2 — The utterance.* The user move is *revise by intent*: "Since the clinic moved into floors 3–4, tenants complain about the lunchtime wait. What would fixing that take?" It is building language only ([elicitation uses the building's vocabulary](../principles/elicit-in-the-buildings-vocabulary.md)), with no parameter named and no parameter asked for.

*F3 — Translation, visibly.* The agent, unasked, maps the complaint to an outcome-term change: tighten the peak wait target from about 40 s to about 25 s. The intended change appears *on the document* as a pending-revision artifact ([Conversation moves](Conversation%20moves.md), *The board and the pieces*), with the affected term highlighted and old and proposed values both visible. Nothing is applied ([revision is an ordinary move, not a restart](../principles/revision-is-an-ordinary-move.md)). The chat carries one line naming the interpretation and inviting correction, and doesn't restate the canvas ([the canvas holds the state and the chat explains it](../principles/canvas-holds-state-chat-explains.md)).

*F4 — The collision.* The solver, involuntarily, derives a rated speed of 2.5 m/s or more from the tightened target, which requires a deeper pit and taller headroom (R03, R04) than modernization of the existing shaft can provide (R27, R28). The revision is infeasible as stated, and the unsat core names exactly these rules plus the recorded choice they collide with, *modernization*. *This frame is where the candidate disclosure levels diverge, and* The divergence *draws each of them.*

*F5 — Repairs as proposals.* The agent narrates the solver's repair options. Each is a complete valid whole ([always show a valid whole](../principles/always-show-a-valid-whole.md)), and each carries what gives, the rules by name ([every refusal names the rules that caused it](../principles/refusals-name-their-rules.md)), and *both* deltas ([trade-offs shown as a pair](../principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md)).

- *Repair A — convert to new build.* Drop modernization, deepen the pit to 2100 mm and raise the headroom to 4600 mm, and the wait target is met in full. Higher monthly price, and materially higher embodied carbon.
- *Repair B — meet the demand partway.* Keep modernization, and settle the wait target at the best value reachable at 1.6 m/s plus a traffic-control change. Small price delta, no construction, no carbon step.
- *Abandon the revision*, always present and always last, as the built repair set already does.

*F6 — The argument.* The user may push back before choosing — "why can't you make it faster?" — and the answer comes from the core (R27: modernization can't deepen the existing pit) rather than being composed. This exchange lives in the transcript and only there, and the repair options themselves sit on the document.

*F7 — Atomic application.* The user picks Repair B. The outcome-term change and the repair land as one solver-validated batch ([revision is an ordinary move, not a restart](../principles/revision-is-an-ordinary-move.md), as built): the wait target *user-chosen*, the rippled values *solver-forced*, and the traffic-control choice *agent-chosen* with its D-rule reason. The pending-revision artifact retires, the canvas shows the new whole, and undo reverses the entire batch in one move.

*F8 — The residue.* The document holds the new state and its provenance, and the transcript holds the argument that produced it. The test for [the canvas holds the state and the chat explains it](../principles/canvas-holds-state-chat-explains.md) applies literally: delete the transcript, and everything the user needs to check is still there.

## 2. The divergence: F4 and F5 at four candidate levels

*Level 0 — the level [nonlinear interaction](../../specs/nonlinear-interaction/requirements.md) built.* The top three repairs by retention, each carrying its ripple filtered to the values that change, with no aggregate price delta, rendered as chat cards. As a disclosure *amount* this is nearly right: deltas per repair rather than everything. Its failure modes are omissions and placement. There is no delta pair, so the two objectives the decision turns on are absent ([trade-offs shown as a pair](../principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md)). And the repair set lives in the transcript, where it scrolls away while still undecided, leaving the artifact the user is deciding *with* on the surface that doesn't retain it ([Surface architecture](Surface%20architecture.md), *What follows from the choice*, [the canvas is the durable locus of state](../assertions/canvas-is-the-durable-state.md)).

*Level A — full consequence set.* Every touched variable, old to new: rated speed 1.6 to 2.5, pit 1600 to 2100 (R03), headroom 3800 to 4600 (R04), installation modernization to new build, plus price and carbon lines — about eight rows at prototype scale. Its failure mode is the named failure of [ripple at the moment of revision](../assertions/ripple-at-the-moment-of-revision.md), where "ripple explanations overwhelm", and of [unsat cores are sufficient for trust](../assertions/cores-are-sufficient-for-trust.md), where "rule-level explanations read as machine noise". The prototype also makes it look better than it is: eight rows are manageable where the real platform's hundreds aren't, so prototype-scale comfort with Level A isn't evidence for it.

*Level B — minimal core.* Only the collision and what has to give: "A 25-second wait needs more speed than an existing shaft allows — the pit and headroom can't be extended in a modernization (R03, R04, R27, R28). One of these has to move: the wait target, or the modernization." Its failure mode is that the user can see *why* it broke but not what fixing it *costs*, and the cost pair is the decision they are there to make ([trade-offs shown as a pair](../principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md)). Bare Level B pushes the user to interrogate each repair, which is the question sequence [showing a candidate works better than asking a sequence of questions](../assertions/candidate-works-better-than-questions.md) exists to avoid.

*Level C — narrated summary.* Prose only, in chat: "Unfortunately that speed isn't possible in your building without major work; I'd suggest…". It fails three ways. The justification is only as trustworthy as its composition, which is exactly what [every refusal names the rules that caused it](../principles/refusals-name-their-rules.md) rules out. It duplicates the canvas's job ([the canvas holds the state and the chat explains it](../principles/canvas-holds-state-chat-explains.md)). And it leaves nothing scannable for later review ([the canvas is the durable locus of state](../assertions/canvas-is-the-durable-state.md)). Level C isn't really a disclosure level, but the pure-chat failure the project frames against.

## 3. The decision — the built spine, completed and relocated

The chosen form keeps Level 0's disclosure amount and repairs its omissions, and the layers map onto the surfaces.

- *By default, at the moment of revision:* the core in plain words with its named rules, which is Level B, plus repairs with their ripple filtered to deltas, which is Level 0's spine. Each repair carries *both* deltas, price and carbon ([trade-offs shown as a pair](../principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md)). This is what the user needs to choose with, and nothing more.
- *One move away:* Level A, per repair. Expanding a repair shows its full consequence set as a document diff, so depth is available on demand and never shown by default.
- *Placement:* the pending revision and its repair set are document-side artifacts that retire on resolution. The chat narrates the *why* only, paraphrasing named rules, which demotes Level C to the narration channel [the canvas holds the state and the chat explains it](../principles/canvas-holds-state-chat-explains.md) already assigns it, and never the carrier of the ripple.

Why this and not the others as the default: it matches the decision the user actually faces, which is which repair to take; it is the only level that holds up when the scale argument is applied; and it keeps the surface division clean, with the chat explaining and the canvas holding the state. Level 0 unamended fails twice by omission. It shows no delta pair, so the cost-and-carbon trade-off the decision turns on is invisible ([trade-offs shown as a pair](../principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md)), and it leaves the undecided repair set in the transcript rather than on the canvas ([the canvas is the durable locus of state](../assertions/canvas-is-the-durable-state.md)). A-first was rejected for overwhelm and for flattering the prototype's scale, and C-only for trust and duplication.

## 4. The second test: reviewing a delegated batch

[Conversation moves](Conversation%20moves.md), *Delegation moves*, requires the chosen disclosure to also serve *review* of delegated agent fills, which is a diff of eleven interior values rather than one revision. Per-item minimal cores don't transfer, because eleven cores read as noise. The layering does transfer if the grouping unit changes: the review pass groups *agent-chosen* values by the D-rule that produced them — "standard interior for retail traffic: five values" — with each group carrying its reason and the full value list one move down. That is reason first and detail on demand, the same shape as *The decision*. If grouping by D-rule can't carry a real batch, this decision reopens.

## 5. Failure signals for the walkthrough

These predictions were made before the 2026-08-20 walkthrough, which found the mechanism working and the disclosure thin ([ripple at the moment of revision](../assertions/ripple-at-the-moment-of-revision.md), [mid-contract revision](../scenarios/mid-contract-revision.md)). They stand for the walkthrough that follows the next cycle of building.

- Every repair gets expanded every time. The B default is too terse, and the balance shifts toward A.
- The user re-asks in chat what the cards already show. The named-rule paraphrase undershoots the narrative demand, which is the failure mode [unsat cores are sufficient for trust](../assertions/cores-are-sufficient-for-trust.md) names.
- Repair cards are ignored and a fresh configuration started. The claim that [showing the ripple at the moment of revision makes nonlinear change workable](../assertions/ripple-at-the-moment-of-revision.md) fails outright, and the novelty claim with it.
- Comfort with full-set disclosure at 23 variables must not be recorded as evidence for Level A. The scale caveat stands.

## 6. What this changes downstream

- The chosen layering gives content to the amendment [Surface architecture](Surface%20architecture.md), *What follows from the choice*, already marks as pending on the [canvas](../../specs/agreement-document/requirements.md) and [nonlinear interaction](../../specs/nonlinear-interaction/requirements.md): the pending-revision artifact and repair set move document-side, and each repair gains the delta pair and a full-set expansion. That is spec work, citing this model and [ripple at the moment of revision](../assertions/ripple-at-the-moment-of-revision.md), to be written after the model survives critique. The delta pair has both halves to render from: the monthly-price terms of the [service agreement](../../specs/service-agreement/requirements.md), and the data of the [footprint spec](../../specs/environmental-footprint/requirements.md).
- The [comparison view](Comparison%20view.md) inherits the same layering as a hypothesis — differing variables first, full sheets one move down — to be tested against the alternatives [trade-offs shown as a pair](../principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md) allows, rather than assumed. Both sides of it are full documents ([parallel drafts](../../specs/parallel-drafts/requirements.md)), so "full sheets one move down" is a reachable place rather than a figure of speech.
- One configuration with history against several live candidates ([problem framing](../problem-framing.md), *Open questions the framing does not settle*) is settled the other way from this storyboard's lean: a workspace holds several live drafts, one current ([parallel drafts](../../specs/parallel-drafts/requirements.md)). F3's pending-revision artifact survives the decision rather than losing to it, because the two are different objects at different scales. A pending revision is one proposed change to the current draft, shown before it is applied and retired on resolution. A draft is a whole alternative agreement that is worked on rather than resolved. The storyboard's frames are unchanged: F3 through F7 all happen within one draft, and forking is what the operator does instead when they want to keep the pre-revision agreement rather than undo back to it.
