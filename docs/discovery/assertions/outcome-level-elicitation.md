# Outcome-level elicitation works

People can specify an agreement in outcome terms — handling capacity, uptime, response time, term, monthly price — and let hardware be derived beneath.

*Evidence today.* Tacton's needs-based practice ([configuration field](../../research/configuration-field.md)), plus the [service frame](../../specs/service-agreement/requirements.md), which gives elicitation a natural top layer the product frame lacked.

*How we would know it is wrong.* Users insist on specifying hardware directly, or can't judge outcome terms without seeing hardware first.

*Status.* It held in the 2026-08-20 walkthrough, at least on the system's side. Every scenario was specified from the outcome layer — building, floors, traffic, service level, term — and the hardware was derived beneath it and shown in the schedules with plain-language glosses. Whether people can judge those outcome terms without seeing the hardware first is exactly what an author-run pass can't answer.

*What rests on it.* The derived layer beneath the outcome terms, and the agent's licence to fill it unasked. That delegation is what this assertion claims ([Conversation moves](../models/Conversation%20moves.md), *The board and the pieces* and *Solver moves*).
