# Showing the ripple at the moment of revision makes nonlinear change workable

Nonlinear revision becomes workable when the ripple is shown *at the moment of revision*, with repairs proposed.

This is the project's central claim. If it fails, the project loses its novelty.

*Evidence today.* It is the design bet of the [nonlinear-interaction spec](../../specs/nonlinear-interaction/requirements.md), with no evidence yet. Frame tracking solved parallel hypothetical states a decade ago and never got an LLM-era UI, so there is nothing to retrieve, and [frame tracking without a UI](../../research/gaps.md#frame-tracking-without-a-ui) is being answered by building.

*How we would know it is wrong.* Ripple explanations are ignored or overwhelm, or users prefer to start a fresh configuration over repairing one.

*Status.* The 2026-08-20 walkthrough showed the mechanism working and the disclosure thin. The ripple did arrive at the moment of revision, with repairs proposed and the option of changing nothing, and applying one landed atomically while undo reversed the whole batch. But the card named only the rules of the collision, not of the nine consequences it listed. It carried no price and no footprint for the path it proposed, so the customer chose between paths without either objective. And for a twelve-floor building it offered a travel height of 75–100 m as an ordinary consequence, trading a measured fact about the building as though it were a preference.

The claim stands as feasible and untested. Two of those three were repaired the same day — declining a repair can no longer move the agreement, and applying one reprices rather than leaving the sheet unpriced — while the disclosure faults, the unnamed ripple rules and the missing delta pair, are the storyboard's open amendment.

*What rests on it.* The whole [ripple storyboard](../models/Ripple%20storyboard.md), and review of delegated agent work, which reuses the same machinery ([Conversation moves](../models/Conversation%20moves.md), *Delegation moves*).
