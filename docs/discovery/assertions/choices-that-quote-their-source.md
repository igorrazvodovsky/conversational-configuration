# A choice that quotes its source can be revisited without re-arguing attribution

A recorded choice should carry its grounds as words: the customer's quoted utterance, or the document's quoted clause, rather than only a source category. When the grounds are one look away, revisiting a value becomes a decision about substance — "is 1000 kg still right?" — instead of an argument about attribution — "did I ever say that?". Delegating the rest of the sheet to the agent stays tolerable because what was actually stated can be checked.

This supports the direction's central tension. [The agent proposes and the user decides](../principles/agent-proposes-user-decides.md) is reconciled with [always show a valid whole](../principles/always-show-a-valid-whole.md) through visible provenance plus cheap reversal, and a three-way source badge is the weakest available reading of visible provenance. The claim here is that the badge is insufficient, and that the words themselves are what people work with.

It also closes a gap in [the canvas is the durable locus of state](canvas-is-the-durable-state.md). Conversations are ephemeral by design, several per workspace, so words that live only in the transcript are lost to the durable record. That would reintroduce the transcript re-litigation problem of pure chat ([problem framing](../problem-framing.md), *Problem statement*, why #4) one level up.

*Evidence today.* The document channel already implements this. RFQ requirements carry clause and quote, frozen in state and rendered in the badge popover, which is what makes the deviation register readable as the customer's own ask rather than the vendor's paraphrase ([a document-seeded candidate with named deviations is better than manual compliance checking](seeded-candidate-with-named-deviations.md)). Tender practice agrees: a compliance matrix cites clauses, and a bare "complies" isn't accepted. The intent-communication literature (Kim et al., CHI 2025) names *management* — stated preferences scattering across conversation turns — as a distinct failure mode of chat-only interfaces, remedied by externalising stated intent as persistent objects. The prose channel is untested here, and the [choice-provenance spec](../../specs/choice-provenance/requirements.md) exists to test it.

*How we would know it is wrong.* The popover goes unopened, which would mean the badge alone carries all the trust the customer needs and the words are ceremony. Or extraction fidelity is too low: the agent paraphrases, and a misattributed "quote" is worse than a bare badge, because it puts words in the customer's mouth. The RFQ verification already logged one invented mapping, reading clause 5.2 as a requirement the document left open.

*Status.* The 2026-08-20 walkthrough exercised both halves of the gap this note names. Document-seeded terms quoted their clause, and the deviation on the sheet read as the customer's own ask. Terms stated in prose carried the badge and nothing else, and when the customer asked why one of them was there, the agent reconstructed a reason from current state rather than quoting anything that had been said. The walkthrough also found a way for words to be put in the customer's mouth that this note didn't anticipate: clicking a card wrote the catalogue's codes into the transcript as the customer's own sentence.

*What rests on it.* The [choice-provenance spec](../../specs/choice-provenance/requirements.md), and the provenance half of the test in [the agent proposes and the user decides](../principles/agent-proposes-user-decides.md), which sharpens from *who chose a value* to *on what words*. The streaming generalisation — a stated-needs register diffing every channel's asks against the live agreement — depends on this assertion holding, and on the reference-semantics question recorded in [problem framing](../problem-framing.md) under *Open questions the framing does not settle*.
