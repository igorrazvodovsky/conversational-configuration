# An agreement that carries its open points can be resumed without rereading the conversation

The document holds the state ([the canvas, rather than the transcript, is the durable locus of state](canvas-is-the-durable-state.md)), but state is what has been decided. What remains *unanswered* is scattered: a pending deviation sits inside the register, an unpriced draft shows only by a missing figure, and whether the agreement has been taken at all is recorded nowhere. The claim: when the document itself carries a short standing list of its open points — each a question some party still has to answer, present only while the move that closes it is available — a returning operator orients from the document alone, and the transcript stays what it is, an ephemeral view. Contract negotiation already keeps this artifact: an issues list beside the draft, whose emptying is what concluding the negotiation means.

The boundary is most of the claim. An open point is a question someone must answer, never a blank the interface would like filled. Undecided variables are not open points — [always show a valid whole](../principles/always-show-a-valid-whole.md) means the candidate answers them — and a list of unfilled slots would rebuild, at the document's edge, the interrogation that [showing a candidate works better than asking a sequence of questions](candidate-works-better-than-questions.md) exists to reject.

*Evidence today.* Genre practice — issues lists in contract negotiation, clarification logs in tendering — plus reasoning. The task-model literature supplies the framing (a goal is what persists across sessions; its completion condition is what retires it) but no evidence for this surface. Untested here.

*How we would know it is wrong.* A returning operator shown the open points still rereads the transcript, or asks the agent to summarize, before acting — the list is not what orientation needs. Or the opposite failure: the list reads as a checklist to clear, operators work to empty it, and the wizard has returned at the document's edge. Either falsifies it; over-selection is the likelier one.

*What rests on it.* The [open-points spec](../../specs/open-points/requirements.md) — the derivation, the acceptance mark, and the document's rendering of both. The session map ([phase-plan.md](../phase-plan.md), next cycle) is the presentation this substrate exists to feed; if the assertion fails, the substrate remains useful only for the agent's own orientation, and the session map needs a different basis.
