# Environmental footprint

Status: requirements approved 2026-08-13, as revised against the footprint research notes in [docs/research/](../../research/README.md). Implemented and browser-passed 2026-08-13.

Grounding: an elevator's life-cycle impact splits into two parts that behave differently in a configuration model. *Embodied* impact (materials, manufacturing) is roughly additive per chosen option. *Use-phase* energy is not additive: it depends on interactions between drive, speed, energy features, and how the building actually uses the elevator (the framing of ISO 25745-2, which assigns usage categories and energy classes) — across the ISO 25745 traffic range its share of the lifetime total moves from roughly a quarter to three quarters, so the split is a property of the product, the building and the grid together ([use-phase-energy.md](../../research/footprint/use-phase-energy.md)). Making footprint a first-class decision dimension in a configuration *conversation* — visible alongside price, queryable, tradeable — is the research angle.

Serves discovery principle [trade-offs shown as a pair](../../discovery/principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md) and puts [two objectives held as a pair](../../discovery/assertions/two-objectives-as-a-pair.md) under test. Evidence for both is now in [docs/research/footprint/sustainability-prior-art.md](../../research/footprint/sustainability-prior-art.md) rather than assumed.

This is the product model's first schema evolution beyond "options carry one price scalar," so the modelling shape was the decision approval settled.

## Modelling decisions

1. *Embodied carbon as an option-level scalar, on more variables than price.* Each option gains a `co2` field (kg CO₂e delta), summed by the service and available as a second optimization objective ("greenest valid completion"). Unlike `price`, `co2` must also be carried by variables that are currently price-free — notably `travel` and `stops`, which together drive guide rails, roping, travelling cable and landing door sets, the largest embodied terms in the model. A `co2` field that mirrored `price` exactly would report a 100 m tower lift and a 15 m residential lift as embodied-identical. Both are banded enums already, so this costs nothing structurally; it just breaks the symmetry. The research grounds it: embodied carbon does not track price — travel height dominates every other embodied term at roughly 85–100 kg CO₂e per metre of shaft, one travel band outweighing every finish option in the model combined, while the striking-looking stainless-vs-glass inversion rests on an *assumed* stainless factor and is encoded as a hypothesis rather than designed on ([embodied-carbon.md](../../research/footprint/embodied-carbon.md) §3). Values are illustrative with plausible relative magnitudes, derived bottom-up (assumed component mass × published material factor) per the same note.
2. *Use-phase as a derived enum, not arithmetic.* A new `energy_class` variable (A–D, ISO 25745-flavoured) is *determined* by table constraints over drive and energy package — the same mechanism as every other derived value in the model. A small data table gives annual kWh per (energy class, usage profile, travel band) — travel must be a key, or conditional regeneration (decision 3) cannot have its evidence-mandated shape; the service multiplies out lifetime energy × a single grid factor, the same trivial arithmetic it already does for price totals. Rejected alternative: a numeric/arithmetic constraint layer in the solver — more faithful physics, but it breaks the enum-only schema shape (the [product-model design](../product-model/design.md)) and gains nothing for the interaction research.
3. *The energy tables must reproduce two shapes, not just plausible magnitudes.* The evidence is unanimous on both, and a table that misses them will make the interaction demonstrate something false:
   - *Standby inversion.* Standby is 44–90% of consumption at low usage and around 10% at high traffic ([use-phase-energy.md](../../research/footprint/use-phase-energy.md) §4). An eco lighting/standby package must therefore be a large lever on a low-traffic residential unit and a small one on a busy office unit.
   - *Conditional regeneration.* A regenerative drive's benefit scales with travel height and traffic, because regeneration only recovers energy when the machine is overhauled. A flat percentage saving is the wrong shape. Hydraulic cannot regenerate at all.
4. *New sustainability-relevant choices.* An `energy_package` variable (standard / eco lighting + standby / regenerative drive) with coupling constraints: regenerative drive requires a gearless traction platform. This constraint is evidence-backed rather than arbitrary: measured comparisons put a gearless PMSM's regenerated power an order of magnitude above a geared induction machine's ([use-phase-energy.md](../../research/footprint/use-phase-energy.md) §6). Without at least one variable the user can *steer*, footprint would be a passive readout rather than a decision dimension.
5. *Fixed assessment assumptions, with sourced defaults.* Named, inspectable assumptions in the model data — not user variables (except usage profile, shared with the [service-agreement spec](../service-agreement/requirements.md)):
   - Service life *25 years* — declared by both KONE EPDs.
   - Grid factor *0.22 kg CO₂e/kWh*, fixed, European average — matches both EPD-derived Belgian factors and the EEA 2024 estimate. A decarbonising alternative is exposed in the assumptions panel rather than hidden, per RICS whole-life-carbon practice of running scenarios as bookends.
   - *365 operating days/year* — declared by both EPDs.
   - Usage profile per building type, mapped onto ISO 25745 categories: residential UC1–2, hotel UC3, office UC4, retail UC4–5, hospital UC4.
   - A *fabrication multiplier* (~1.8–2.0) applied to bottom-up embodied estimates so a reference configuration reconciles to the EPD anchors (~8.5 t A1–A3 at 630 kg/12 m, ~21 t at 1600 kg/14 m). Raw material factors alone underestimate by about half, because EPDs include electronics, cabling, fabrication energy and losses.
6. *Claims discipline as a model-level and UI-level constraint.* The numbers are illustrative, which imposes obligations rather than relaxing them — EU Directive 2024/825 (applying from 27 September 2026) bans generic environmental claims and sustainability labels not backed by a certification scheme, and a self-styled A–G badge is in scope even for a prototype:
   - `energy_class` is presented as *modelled, ISO 25745-flavoured* — never as an achieved or certified class, and never with EU-energy-label styling. (The real A–G boundaries are paywalled and were deliberately not guessed; [use-phase-energy.md](../../research/footprint/use-phase-energy.md) §5.)
   - Footprint is shown *cumulatively* for the whole configuration. No per-option carbon badges: most option-level deltas are noise against a total in the tens of tonnes, and the carbon-labelling literature documents moral licensing — an early green choice licences worse later ones — which is acute across a sequence of twenty choices. Cumulative total plus paired comparison is both the better-evidenced format and the one [trade-offs shown as a pair](../../discovery/principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md) already asks for.
   - The assumptions panel names service life, usage profile, grid factor, module scope, and states plainly that this is an illustrative model, not a verified assessment. The agent can surface it on request in chat, which is where an unhedged sentence is most likely to escape.
   - Footprint is never described as "green", "eco-friendly" or "sustainable"; comparative and conditional phrasing only ("lower modelled footprint than the alternative, under these assumptions").

### Considered and deferred

*Green component variable value ordering* (Felfernig et al., ConfWS 2025) — biasing the solver's search order toward lower-carbon options so greener candidates surface first. Deferred, and settled as staying deferred in [design.md](design.md): a green search bias is exactly the silent discretionary preference [the agent proposes and the user decides](../../discovery/principles/agent-proposes-user-decides.md) forbids, admissible only with a disclosed-provenance design that does not exist.

## Stories

- As a user configuring an elevator, I see the footprint of the current candidate — embodied plus estimated use-phase, with the assumptions inspectable — alongside its price, updating as my choices change, so environmental impact is a dimension of the decision rather than a report after it.
- As a user, I ask footprint questions in the conversation ("how much CO₂ does the panoramic glass wall add?", "what's the greenest option that still meets my needs?") and every number in the answer traces to model data or a solver computation — the agent never estimates impact on its own (constitution #1, #6).
- As a user weighing cost against footprint, I hold the cheapest and the greenest valid completions of my current choices side by side and see exactly which options differ and both deltas.
- As a user who has been told a number, I can ask what it assumes and get service life, usage profile, grid factor and module scope, stated as assumptions rather than facts.
- As the developer, I change an option's footprint or the energy tables by editing the declarative model only (constitution #2) — no solver or agent code changes.

## Acceptance criteria

- GIVEN the extended model, WHEN the validator runs, THEN the `energy_class` and `energy_package` variables and their coupling constraints pass the same satisfiability and dead-option checks as all other variables.
- GIVEN the extended model, WHEN the validator runs, THEN every option of every variable carries a `co2` value — including `travel` and `stops`, which carry no `price` — and the validator fails on a missing one.
- GIVEN a partial configuration, WHEN a completion is requested with the footprint objective, THEN the solver returns a valid completion minimizing lifetime CO₂e, symmetric with the existing cheapest-completion behavior.
- GIVEN a candidate on the canvas, WHEN its footprint is displayed, THEN it is broken down into embodied and use-phase parts, and the assumptions behind the use-phase estimate (usage profile, service life, grid factor, module scope) are visible on demand.
- GIVEN the energy tables, WHEN a low-traffic and a high-traffic usage profile are compared, THEN the eco lighting/standby package saves proportionally more on the low-traffic profile, and the regenerative drive saves proportionally more on the high-traffic and higher-travel profile.
- GIVEN an eco choice that conflicts with prior choices (e.g. regenerative drive on the hydraulic platform), WHEN the conflict occurs, THEN it is explained via named rules like any other constraint (constitution #6, [every refusal names the rules that caused it](../../discovery/principles/refusals-name-their-rules.md)).
- GIVEN cheapest and greenest completions of the same partial configuration, WHEN compared, THEN the comparison lists only differing variables with correct price and footprint deltas.
- GIVEN any footprint number the agent states in chat, THEN it originates from model data or a solver/service computation, never from the LLM.
- GIVEN any surface showing footprint, THEN `energy_class` is labelled as modelled rather than certified, no individual option carries a carbon badge, and no configuration is described with a generic environmental claim.

## Consequences for existing specs

- [Product model](../product-model/requirements.md): schema evolves — options carry `{price, co2}` with `co2` also on price-free variables, new variables `energy_package` and `energy_class` with coupling tables, a use-phase energy data section, and named assessment assumptions including the fabrication multiplier; the validator checks the new fields and enforces `co2` completeness.
- [Solver service](../solver-service/requirements.md): `complete()` gains a footprint objective; the service computes embodied and lifetime use-phase totals alongside price.
- [Agent tools](../agent-tools/requirements.md) and [canvas](../agreement-document/requirements.md): configuration state and spec-sheet gain a cumulative footprint summary and an assumptions view; tool surface expected otherwise unchanged.
- [Comparing agreements](../../discovery/scenarios/comparing-agreements.md) (built; the harness asserts the footprint delta beside the price delta and that no key combines them) is where [trade-offs shown as a pair](../../discovery/principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md) and [two objectives held as a pair](../../discovery/assertions/two-objectives-as-a-pair.md) get demonstrated; it should show a case where price and footprint disagree. Use *travel band vs. any finish* — that case rests on the retrieved steel factor and a 1.5–6× magnitude gap, so it holds regardless of the material-factor uncertainties ([gaps.md](../../research/gaps.md#e4)). The stainless-vs-glass case is more striking but depends on an unsourced factor; do not build a demo on it.
- [Service agreement](../service-agreement/requirements.md) (implemented): introduced the shared `usage_profile` variable, which this spec's energy tables reconcile with. Footprint uses a fixed assumed service life, deliberately independent of contract term. The two specs meet at two further points now that footprint has landed: the agreement comparison shows the monthly-price and footprint deltas side by side ([trade-offs are shown as a pair](../../discovery/principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md)), and the first proposal's objective — the standing tension in [the conversation move inventory](../../discovery/models/Conversation%20moves.md) §6 — must be settled rather than defaulting to cheapest.
- Discovery: [two objectives held as a pair](../../discovery/assertions/two-objectives-as-a-pair.md) no longer rests on assumption alone; its evidence line is updated in the same revision as this spec.

## Out of scope

Real EPD/LCA data (illustrative numbers with plausible relative magnitudes suffice); EN 15804 module-level granularity as *computation* — the module mapping is named in the assumptions panel per decision 6, but A1–A3/B6/C are not modelled or reported separately; end-of-life and circularity modelling; regionalized grid factors; energy simulation or telemetry; any certification or compliance claim about the numbers.
