# Environmental footprint

Status: requirements approved 2026-08-13, as revised against the [footprint research notes](../../research/README.md). Implemented and browser-passed 2026-08-13.

Grounding: an elevator's life-cycle impact splits into two parts that behave differently in a configuration model. *Embodied* impact, from materials and manufacturing, is roughly additive per chosen option. *Use-phase* energy isn't additive. It depends on interactions between drive, speed, energy features, and how the building actually uses the elevator — the framing of ISO 25745-2, which assigns usage categories and energy classes. Across the ISO 25745 traffic range, the use-phase share of the lifetime total moves from roughly a quarter to three quarters, so the split is a property of the product, the building and the grid together ([how use-phase energy behaves](../../research/footprint/use-phase-energy.md)). The research angle is making footprint a first-class decision dimension in a configuration *conversation*: visible alongside price, queryable, tradeable.

Serves discovery principle [trade-offs shown as a pair](../../discovery/principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md) and puts [two objectives held as a pair](../../discovery/assertions/two-objectives-as-a-pair.md) under test. Evidence for both is in [the carbon-presentation prior art](../../research/footprint/sustainability-prior-art.md) rather than assumed.

This is the product model's first schema evolution beyond "options carry one price scalar", so the modelling shape was what the decision approval settled.

## Modelling decisions

1. *Embodied carbon as an option-level scalar, on more variables than price.* Each option gains a `co2` field, a kg CO₂e delta, summed by the service and available as a second optimization objective, the greenest valid completion. Unlike `price`, `co2` also has to be carried by variables that are price-free, notably `travel` and `stops`, which together drive guide rails, roping, travelling cable and landing door sets — the largest embodied terms in the model. A `co2` field that mirrored `price` exactly would report a 100 m tower lift and a 15 m residential lift as embodied-identical. Both are banded enums already, so this costs nothing structurally and only breaks the symmetry. The research grounds it: embodied carbon doesn't track price. Travel height dominates every other embodied term at roughly 85–100 kg CO₂e per metre of shaft, with one travel band outweighing every finish option in the model combined, while the striking-looking stainless-versus-glass inversion rests on an *assumed* stainless factor and is encoded as a hypothesis rather than designed on ([bottom-up embodied carbon](../../research/footprint/embodied-carbon.md), *Car finishes*). Values are illustrative with plausible relative magnitudes, derived bottom-up from assumed component mass times published material factor, per the same note.
2. *Use-phase as a derived enum, not arithmetic.* A new `energy_class` variable, A–D and ISO 25745-flavoured, is *determined* by table constraints over drive and energy package, the same mechanism as every other derived value in the model. A small data table gives annual kWh per energy class, usage profile and travel band. Travel has to be a key, or conditional regeneration in decision 3 can't have its evidence-mandated shape. The service multiplies out lifetime energy by a single grid factor, the same trivial arithmetic it already does for price totals. The rejected alternative was a numeric or arithmetic constraint layer in the solver: more faithful physics, but it breaks the enum-only schema shape (the [product-model design](../product-model/design.md)) and gains nothing for the interaction research.
3. *The energy tables have to reproduce two shapes, not only plausible magnitudes.* The evidence is unanimous on both, and a table that misses them will make the interaction demonstrate something false.
   - *Standby inversion.* Standby is 44–90% of consumption at low usage and around 10% at high traffic ([how use-phase energy behaves](../../research/footprint/use-phase-energy.md), *Worked examples*). An eco lighting and standby package therefore has to be a large lever on a low-traffic residential unit and a small one on a busy office unit.
   - *Conditional regeneration.* A regenerative drive's benefit scales with travel height and traffic, because regeneration only recovers energy when the machine is overhauled. A flat percentage saving is the wrong shape, and hydraulic can't regenerate at all.
4. *New sustainability-relevant choices.* An `energy_package` variable — standard, eco lighting and standby, or regenerative drive — with coupling constraints: a regenerative drive requires a gearless traction platform. That constraint is evidence-backed rather than arbitrary, because measured comparisons put a gearless PMSM's regenerated power an order of magnitude above a geared induction machine's ([how use-phase energy behaves](../../research/footprint/use-phase-energy.md), *Regenerative drives*). Without at least one variable the user can *steer*, footprint would be a passive readout rather than a decision dimension.
5. *Fixed assessment assumptions, with sourced defaults.* These are named, inspectable assumptions in the model data rather than user variables, the one exception being usage profile, shared with the [service-agreement spec](../service-agreement/requirements.md).
   - Service life of *25 years*, declared by both KONE EPDs.
   - A fixed grid factor of *0.22 kg CO₂e/kWh*, a European average, matching both EPD-derived Belgian factors and the EEA 2024 estimate. A decarbonising alternative is exposed in the assumptions panel rather than hidden, following RICS whole-life-carbon practice of running scenarios as bookends.
   - *365 operating days per year*, declared by both EPDs.
   - Usage profile per building type, mapped onto ISO 25745 categories: residential UC1–2, hotel UC3, office UC4, retail UC4–5, hospital UC4.
   - A *fabrication multiplier* of about 1.8–2.0, applied to bottom-up embodied estimates so a reference configuration reconciles to the EPD anchors: about 8.5 t A1–A3 at 630 kg and 12 m, and about 21 t at 1600 kg and 14 m. Raw material factors alone underestimate by about half, because EPDs include electronics, cabling, fabrication energy and losses.
6. *Claims discipline as a model-level and UI-level constraint.* The numbers are illustrative, which imposes obligations rather than relaxing them. EU Directive 2024/825, which applies from 27 September 2026, bans generic environmental claims and sustainability labels not backed by a certification scheme, and a self-styled A–G badge is in scope even for a prototype.
   - `energy_class` is presented as *modelled, ISO 25745-flavoured* — never as an achieved or certified class, and never with EU-energy-label styling. The real A–G boundaries are paywalled and were deliberately not guessed ([how use-phase energy behaves](../../research/footprint/use-phase-energy.md), *Classification A–G*).
   - Footprint is shown *cumulatively* for the whole configuration. There are no per-option carbon badges: most option-level deltas are noise against a total in the tens of tonnes, and the carbon-labelling literature documents moral licensing, where an early green choice licences worse later ones, which is acute across a sequence of twenty choices. Cumulative total plus paired comparison is both the better-evidenced format and the one [trade-offs shown as a pair](../../discovery/principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md) already asks for.
   - The assumptions panel names service life, usage profile, grid factor and module scope, and states plainly that this is an illustrative model rather than a verified assessment. The agent can surface it on request in chat, which is where an unhedged sentence is most likely to escape.
   - Footprint is never described as "green", "eco-friendly" or "sustainable". Phrasing is comparative and conditional only: "lower modelled footprint than the alternative, under these assumptions".

### Considered and deferred

*Green component variable value ordering*, from Felfernig et al., ConfWS 2025, biases the solver's search order toward lower-carbon options so greener candidates surface first. It is deferred, and [design.md](design.md) settled that it stays deferred: a green search bias is exactly the silent discretionary preference [the agent proposes and the user decides](../../discovery/principles/agent-proposes-user-decides.md) forbids, admissible only with a disclosed-provenance design that doesn't exist.

## Stories

- As a user configuring an elevator, I see the footprint of the current candidate — embodied plus estimated use-phase, with the assumptions inspectable — alongside its price, updating as my choices change, so environmental impact is a dimension of the decision rather than a report after it.
- As a user, I ask footprint questions in the conversation — "how much CO₂ does the panoramic glass wall add?", "what's the greenest option that still meets my needs?" — and every number in the answer traces to model data or a solver computation. The agent never estimates impact on its own (constitution #1, #6).
- As a user weighing cost against footprint, I hold the cheapest and the greenest valid completions of my current choices side by side and see exactly which options differ and both deltas.
- As a user who has been told a number, I can ask what it assumes and get service life, usage profile, grid factor and module scope, stated as assumptions rather than facts.
- As the developer, I change an option's footprint or the energy tables by editing the declarative model only (constitution #2), with no solver or agent code changes.

## Acceptance criteria

- GIVEN the extended model, WHEN the validator runs, THEN the `energy_class` and `energy_package` variables and their coupling constraints pass the same satisfiability and dead-option checks as all other variables.
- GIVEN the extended model, WHEN the validator runs, THEN every option of every variable carries a `co2` value, including `travel` and `stops`, which carry no `price`, and the validator fails on a missing one.
- GIVEN a partial configuration, WHEN a completion is requested with the footprint objective, THEN the solver returns a valid completion minimizing lifetime CO₂e, symmetric with the existing cheapest-completion behavior.
- GIVEN a candidate on the canvas, WHEN its footprint is displayed, THEN it is broken down into embodied and use-phase parts, and the assumptions behind the use-phase estimate — usage profile, service life, grid factor, module scope — are visible on demand.
- GIVEN the energy tables, WHEN a low-traffic and a high-traffic usage profile are compared, THEN the eco lighting and standby package saves proportionally more on the low-traffic profile, and the regenerative drive saves proportionally more on the high-traffic, higher-travel profile.
- GIVEN an eco choice that conflicts with prior choices, such as a regenerative drive on the hydraulic platform, WHEN the conflict occurs, THEN it is explained through named rules like any other constraint (constitution #6, [every refusal names the rules that caused it](../../discovery/principles/refusals-name-their-rules.md)).
- GIVEN cheapest and greenest completions of the same partial configuration, WHEN compared, THEN the comparison lists only differing variables with correct price and footprint deltas.
- GIVEN any footprint number the agent states in chat, THEN it originates from model data or a solver or service computation, never from the LLM.
- GIVEN any surface showing footprint, THEN `energy_class` is labelled as modelled rather than certified, no individual option carries a carbon badge, and no configuration is described with a generic environmental claim.

## Consequences for existing specs

- [Product model](../product-model/requirements.md): the schema evolves. Options carry `{price, co2}`, with `co2` also on price-free variables; new variables `energy_package` and `energy_class` arrive with coupling tables; a use-phase energy data section and named assessment assumptions, including the fabrication multiplier, are added. The validator checks the new fields and enforces `co2` completeness.
- [Solver service](../solver-service/requirements.md): `complete()` gains a footprint objective, and the service computes embodied and lifetime use-phase totals alongside price.
- [Agent tools](../agent-tools/requirements.md) and the [canvas](../agreement-document/requirements.md): configuration state and the spec sheet gain a cumulative footprint summary and an assumptions view, and the tool surface is otherwise expected to be unchanged.
- [Comparing agreements](../../discovery/scenarios/comparing-agreements.md), which is built, and whose harness asserts the footprint delta beside the price delta and that no key combines them, is where [trade-offs shown as a pair](../../discovery/principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md) and [two objectives held as a pair](../../discovery/assertions/two-objectives-as-a-pair.md) get demonstrated. It should show a case where price and footprint disagree. Use *travel band against any finish*: that case rests on the retrieved steel factor and a 1.5–6× magnitude gap, so it holds regardless of the material-factor uncertainties ([the assumed material factors](../../research/gaps.md#assumed-material-factors)). The stainless-versus-glass case is more striking but depends on an unsourced factor, so don't build a demo on it.
- [Service agreement](../service-agreement/requirements.md), which is implemented, introduced the shared `usage_profile` variable that this spec's energy tables reconcile with. Footprint uses a fixed assumed service life, deliberately independent of contract term. The two specs meet at two further points now that footprint has landed: the agreement comparison shows the monthly-price and footprint deltas side by side ([trade-offs are shown as a pair](../../discovery/principles/trade-offs-shown-as-a-pair.md)), and the first proposal's objective — the standing tension in [the conversation move inventory](../../discovery/models/Conversation%20moves.md), *The initiative default* — has to be settled rather than defaulting to cheapest.
- Discovery: [two objectives held as a pair](../../discovery/assertions/two-objectives-as-a-pair.md) no longer rests on assumption alone, and its evidence line is updated in the same revision as this spec.

## Out of scope

Real EPD and LCA data, since illustrative numbers with plausible relative magnitudes suffice. EN 15804 module-level granularity as *computation*: the module mapping is named in the assumptions panel per decision 6, but A1–A3, B6 and C aren't modelled or reported separately. End-of-life and circularity modelling. Regionalized grid factors. Energy simulation or telemetry. And any certification or compliance claim about the numbers.
